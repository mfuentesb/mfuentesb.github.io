abstract: >-
  This pre-registration describes a study evaluating how accurately large language models (LLMs) can
  replicate individual-level political attitudes and demographics from social media data. The project
  builds on recent work that used LLM-driven "digital interviews" of social media users to estimate
  public opinion and forecast elections with high aggregate accuracy, but without validation against
  individual-level ground truth. In the present study, conventional survey responses (the ground
  truth) were collected from human participants in Chile between the first and second rounds of the
  2025 Chilean presidential election (field period December 7-13, 2025), alongside each participant's
  X (Twitter) profile data. We will prompt an LLM to answer the same survey questions for each
  participant based solely on that individual's digital trace, under four experimental conditions
  varying access to the participant's social media data and to web search. Comparing AI-generated
  responses to actual survey answers on a per-person basis measures the accuracy with which LLMs infer
  personal attributes and opinions. Critically, the LLM digital interviews have not yet been run: this
  registration is filed after ground-truth collection but before any model predictions are generated
  or compared to survey responses. Because the election outcome is now public, we additionally
  pre-specify information-leakage controls and diagnostics for the web-enabled conditions.

Work in progress, part of the *Talking to Machines* project (Nuffield College, University of Oxford) and its AIPOP (Artificially Intelligent Public Opinion Polling) line. The study asks whether an LLM, given only a person's social media trace, can reproduce that person's own survey answers — vote choice, ideology, and demographics — rather than only matching aggregate election results. Ground truth is a Qualtrics survey fielded in Chile between the two rounds of the 2025 presidential election; the digital interviews are run under a within-subject 2×2 design that varies access to the respondent's posts and to web search. The paper is not yet publicly available.
