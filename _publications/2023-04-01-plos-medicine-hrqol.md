abstract: >-
  Background: Most research on COVID-19 has focused on confirmed cases and deaths rather than
  consequences for general population health-related quality of life (HRQoL). This study assessed
  the association between the pandemic and HRQoL changes across 13 diverse countries.
  Methods and Findings: Adults were surveyed online (November-December 2020) in 13 countries using
  the EQ-5D-5L instrument. The study examined associations between the pandemic and HRQoL changes,
  analyzing individual-level and national-level factors. Results showed overall health deteriorated
  for over one-third of 15,480 participants, primarily in anxiety/depression, especially among those
  under 35 and females/other gender. This represented a 0.066 mean loss (95% CI: -0.075, -0.057;
  p-value &lt; 0.001) in the EQ-5D-5L index, representing a reduction of 8% in overall HRQoL.
  Quality-adjusted life years lost from morbidity were 5-11 times greater than those from COVID-19
  premature mortality. A limitation noted was recall bias from retrospective prepandemic health
  assessments.
  Conclusions: The COVID-19 pandemic was associated with reduced perceived HRQoL globally, especially
  in anxiety/depression and among younger populations. The pandemic's health burden would be
  substantially underestimated based solely on mortality figures. HRQoL measures are essential for
  capturing pandemic-related morbidity in the general population.

Part of the CANDOUR project (Duch lab, Nuffield College, University of Oxford), analyzing how the COVID-19 pandemic affected self-reported health-related quality of life across a diverse set of high- and low-to-middle-income countries.
