abstract: >-
  This pre-registration outlines a study to evaluate how accurately large language models (LLMs) can
  replicate individual-level political attitudes and demographics by analyzing social media data. The
  project builds on recent work that used LLM-driven "digital interviews" of social media users to
  estimate public opinion and forecast elections; those studies demonstrated high aggregate accuracy in
  election predictions, but did not validate predictions against individual-level ground truth survey
  data. In the present study, we collect conventional survey responses (considered the ground truth)
  from human participants in Chile, alongside each participant's social media profile data. We then
  employ an LLM to answer the same survey questions for each participant, based solely on that
  individual's digital trace. By comparing these AI-generated responses to the actual survey answers on
  a per-person basis, we assess the accuracy with which LLMs can infer personal attributes and opinions.
  We further introduce an experimental design with varying conditions for the AI digital interviews
  (varying the availability of the participant's social media data and access to web information) to
  test how additional context affects predictive accuracy. This validation study provides evidence on
  the reliability and limitations of AI-based polling methods at the individual respondent level.

Work in progress, part of the *Talking to Machines* project (Nuffield College, University of Oxford), evaluating whether LLM-based digital-twin interviews can validly reproduce individual-level public opinion, tested against a Chilean election study.
